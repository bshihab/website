ECG PCB

An electrocardiogram board my team designed, built and tested for a BME lab class. It picked up a clean ECG:

  • An AD620 instrumentation amplifier with a gain of about 248
  • A 0.324 Hz high-pass filter to remove baseline drift, and a 39.4 Hz low-pass filter that also cuts 60 Hz mains noise
  • A voltage follower to buffer the output
  • My part was the schematic and the soldering
Collaborated with Diya Arvindh and Emily NetemeyerBME lab, UT AustinMar – Apr 2025

Why I built it

It was the design project for a BME lab class: take a raw heart signal from the skin and build the circuit that makes it readable.

The hard part

The hardware. Everything had to be soldered cleanly, and when the boards arrived, two of our three copies had manufacturing defects. Every time the signal looked wrong, we had to work out whether it was our soldering, a fault in the board itself, or a problem with signal acquisition, and we kept iterating until we had a board that gave the clean trace in Fig. 2.
